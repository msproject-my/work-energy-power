Two Core Forms of Mechanical Energy: Potential & Kinetic

Before studying conservation of energy, understand how energy is stored (Position) and how it manifests in motion (Velocity).

Potential Energy

  • •

    Potential energy, Ep is the energy possessed by an object due to its position.

  • •

    Gravitational potential energy is part of potential energy.

  • •

    The potential energy depends on the mass and height from ground level.

  • •

    Potential energy also referred to as work done against gravity.

  • •

    The unit for potential energy is Nm @ Joule ( J ).

Ep = mgh
m = mass ( kg )
g = gravity ( ms-2 )
h = height from the ground ( m )

Kinetic Energy

  • •

    Kinetic energy, Ek is the energy an object possesses due to its motion.

  • •

    The kinetic energy of a moving object depends on its mass and velocity.

  • •

    The unit for kinetic energy is Nm @ Joule ( J ).

  • •

    The greater the value of mass or velocity, the higher the kinetic energy possessed by an object.

Ek = ½ mv2
m = mass ( kg )
v = velocity ( ms-1 )

Principle of Conservation of Energy

Total Mechanical Energy = Constant (100%)

• The principle of conservation of energy states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed, but can only be transformed from one form to another.

• As the ball travels through points A → B → C → D → E → F, whenever height decreases, Ep converts to Ek; whenever it climbs, Ek converts back to Ep.

📋 Energy Conservation Derivations (Ep & Ek)

Ep + Ek = 100% Invariant
1 Summit A: Total Initial Mechanical Energy h₁ = 6.0m

At rest on summit (Ek = 0 J), all energy is purely gravitational potential:

Ep(A) = m × g × h₁ = 117.72 J • Ek(A) = 0 J → Total Energy = 117.72 J (100%)
2 Valleys B, D & F: Maximum Kinetic Energy h = 0

At the ground datum (Ep = 0 J), potential energy is fully converted into kinetic:

Ep = 0 J → Ek = Total Energy = 117.72 J (100%) → Ep (0%) + Ek (100%) = 100%
3 Mid Crest C: Intermediate Energy Sharing h₂ = 3.8m

Energy is shared between potential and kinetic forms at elevation h₂:

Ep(C) = 74.56 J (63.3%) • Ek(C) = Total − Ep(C) = 43.16 J (36.7%) → Total = 100%
4 Lower Crest E: Low Crest Energy Sharing h₃ = 1.8m

Lower elevation retains higher kinetic energy:

Ep(E) = 35.32 J (30.0%) • Ek(E) = Total − Ep(E) = 82.40 J (70.0%) → Total = 100%
